How we check

Prediction Checker records predictions by scientists, politicians, columnists, executives, forecasters and other well-known voices at the moment they are made. We note who said it, when, where, and a link to the source. Every prediction gets a check date. If a prediction runs for more than two years, we look at how it is doing every year, with a chart where possible.

Correct

The predicted outcome happened. We don't count deviations of 10 to 20% as wrong.

Partly

The core is right but part of it isn't: the direction, say, but not the size or the timing.

Wrong

The outcome is clearly different from what was predicted.

Too early

The target date hasn't arrived yet, or the figures aren't in.

When someone gives several scenarios, we test the one they presented as most likely. A verdict is not a judgement of the person: good experts get it wrong sometimes too, and that is exactly what makes checking interesting.

Tip or correction?

Spotted a prediction we should track, or think a check is wrong? Let us know through the tip form or on X. We check every tip against its source before it goes on the site.
